Bayesian reasoning updates your belief (prior) using new evidence (likelihood) to get an updated belief (posterior).

Bayes's theorem is the rule governing the weight assigned to evidence. In other words, by how much do we revise our probability given a new piece of evidence?

You don't necessarily have to do the explicit math. What might matter more is the underlying principle: updating your beliefs in proportion to the weight of evidence.

<BlockQuote author="Philip Tetlock and Dan Gardner" work="Superforecasting" medium="book">
The superforecasters are a numerate bunch: many know about Bayes' theorem and could deploy it if they felt it was worth the trouble. But they rarely crunch the numbers so explicitly. What matters far more to the superforecasters than Bayes' theorem is Bayes' core insight of gradually getting closer to the truth by constantly updating in proportion to the weight of the evidence.
</BlockQuote>
