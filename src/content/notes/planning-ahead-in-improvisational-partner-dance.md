title: Planning Ahead in Improvisational Partner Dance

Sometimes you're planning ahead, like when you know a break is coming. I don't want to say that's necessary, but it might happen. Or you might just hit a break without really thinking about it, but that won't happen often until you hit a certain level of mastery.

But planning or not, one thing is constant: You're making a lot of decisions, and making them fast. So the one thing that remains true, always, is that **you have to be *on***. It's related to focus, but it's more than focus.

I tend to believe that if you focus on the process, the product will take care of itself.[^2] Applied to dance, that means if you just attend to the connection and the music, good things will happen.[^1]

In [[how-can-we-develop-transformative-tools-for-thought|How Can We Develop Transformative Tools for Thought?]], the authors express a similar idea. They say that no one sat down and thought, "I need to invent the computer." Instead, the computer *emerged* from exploration of basic and fundamental questions, in this case about logic and math.

If we reframe **improvisation as exploration**, then improvisational dance becomes a matter of asking questions. What questions? I can think of one: *What happens if we do this?*

Or maybe the asking should happen off the dance floor, and then every dance is an attempt to answer.

[^1]: Notwithstanding the fact that the range of possibilities is limited by your skill level.
[^2]: First encountered this idea in [[on-writing-well|*On Writing Well*]].
